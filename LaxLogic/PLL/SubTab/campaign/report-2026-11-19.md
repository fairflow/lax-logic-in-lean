## (ii) The statements

**PROVED**, kernel-checked, no `sorry`, within `[propext, Quot.sound]`
(the file ends with `#axioms_within_file [propext, Quot.sound]`):

    (m.rule s).ok = true → Checks (m.rule s).prems cs → check s (m.cert cs) = true     (check_cert_of)
    STab.Sound (fun s => ∃ c, check s c = true)                                         (sound)
    check s (cert d) = true                              for d : Der STab s             (check_cert)
    cert (ofCert c s h) = c                                                             (cert_ofCert)
    Der STab ⟨false, [], [φ]⟩ → Holds φ                                                 (holds_of_der)
    (∃ ls, yesScript.check φ ls = true) ∨ (∃ m, no.check φ m = true)                    (complete_script)

where `Checks ps cs` says that each premise has its closed tableau, and
`Holds φ` is `Nonempty ([] ⊢ φ)`.  Definitions, not theorems: `derivation
d : s.ant ⊢ s.goal` and `term d : [] ⊢ φ`, the natural-deduction proofs of a
derivation, through `deriveOf` and `termOf`.

`complete_script` is from `dichotomy`, which names neither certifier of
`Sound.lean`; `yesScript` is sound through `holds_of_check'`.  So the route
by scripts reaches no constant of FRJW, as `yes'` does not.

**Checked by test, not proved**: on 12 theorems the search's closed tableau
becomes a derivation whose script `derives` accepts and whose tableau is
the one the search found; a corrupted script and a script that stops early
are rejected in the kernel, and the corrupted one fails as a `verdict` term
(watched).

**OPEN**, not attempted: `ofCert (cert d) s _ = d`, the other round trip.

**REFUTED**: nothing.

## (iii) What to look at, and what to try

`Play/SubTab.lean` has seven exercises: the four laws of `◯` (unit,
multiplication, functoriality, strength) and three formulas that are not
theorems.  Each starts with `subtab`, which offers the first move.  The
rule table is in [record.md](record.md) §2.

What should happen:

* a goal is shown as its sequent, `Γ ⇒ Δ` at a world outside S and
  `Γ ⇒ ◯[Δ]` at an S-point;
* in `◯p ↠ p`, after `impR` the query says that no move applies: `boxL`
  needs an S-point, and typed out it answers "the world is not an S-point";
* in `◯(p ∨ q) ↠ (◯p ∨ ◯q)`, `boxR p` leaves `◯q` behind; the branch with
  `q` true stays open, and the query offers only moves already made;
* in Peirce's law the second `impR` leaves the false `p` behind, and the one
  move that then applies leads back to the same sequent.

Reference derivations: `LaxLogic/PLL/SubTab/ExploreTest.lean`.

## (iv) What differed from the plan

1. **The dependency changes.**  proof-tools carries the library
   `LeanCertify` over from lean-certify with the same module names, so a
   project cannot require both.  This branch requires `proof-tools` in
   place of `lean-certify`.  Its `LeanCertify` is lean-certify's `main`,
   later than the tag `v0.3.0` pinned here before.  After the
   change, with their gates and pins unchanged, `lake build LaxLogic FRJGbu
   SubTab CertifyAdoption Harness SubTabTools LaxLogic.QLL.CLPCompare` is
   clean (8828 jobs), and `lake build SubTabExplore` (8680 jobs).
2. **The instance asked nothing new of the library.**  No change to
   `ProofTools.Explore` was needed.  The equation of a node is proved by
   `rfl`: a signed sequent holds no function.
3. **Two checkers of the same proof, timed.**  A closed tableau is checked
   by `check`, a recursion over the tree; its script is checked by
   `derives`, which runs it through the zipper.  On the search's own proofs,
   in the kernel:

   | proof | moves | `check`, the tableau | `derives`, its script |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | strength, 4 atoms | 19 | 11 ms | 26 ms |
   | strength, 8 atoms | 39 | 37 ms | 71 ms |
   | strength, 16 atoms | 79 | 145 ms | 222 ms |
   | strength, 32 atoms | 159 | 618 ms | 788 ms |
   | chain, 4 links | 403 | 69 ms | 498 ms |
   | chain, 8 links | 12,547 | 4.2 s | 20.6 s |

   (The kernel's time for the declaration, from Lean's profiler; one
   machine, 2026-11-19, one run each.)  The recursion over the tree is the
   faster check, by a factor between 1.3 and 7; the script costs about 1 ms
   more for each line.  Where the sequents are long (strength) the
   membership tests dominate both.
4. **The search's proof against a proof by hand.**  The search is
   depth-first and shares nothing between branches.  On a chain of
   implications under `◯`, `(p0 ↠ p1) ↠ … ↠ (◯p0 ↠ ◯pn)`, its closed
   tableau has 403 moves for 4 links and 12,547 for 8; made in the order
   of the chain the proof has 16 moves for 4 links (`chain4Der`,
   `ExploreTest.lean`), three more for each further link.  The checkers
   accept either.
5. **A goal is shown by its reading**, `Γ ⇒ ◯[Δ]` for an S-point, not by
   the Boolean `isS`.
6. **Atoms are written by name** in suggested lines (`p` for `.prop "p"`),
   so a file that uses `subtab` declares its atoms under their names.
7. **Not built yet, and wanted by the full exploration**: after `impR` or
   `boxR` the false formulas left behind are gone from view, and nothing
   says which other demand could have been served.  The tests show the
   effect (the query after `boxR p`).
